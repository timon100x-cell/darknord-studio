# Genre Playbooks

Last checked: 2026-11-12 (source: docs/research.md)

## Country

Country videos keep one coherent story where pop videos fragment. The memorable ones
add a turn the lyric never states.

- Country kept storytelling and genre icons prominent to stay distinct from MTV's
  quick-cut style (Fenster 1988).
- ISMIR 2021, country sample of 34 videos: 29% had a dominant shot length of one bar
  (highest of any genre); only 47% cut at beat level (lowest).
- **For the tool:** bar-paced cutting, and a story a viewer can follow with the sound off.

| Video | What the picture adds | Technique to reuse |
|---|---|---|
| HARDY and Lainey Wilson, "wait in the truck" (dir. Justin Clough) | Stages the lyric scene by scene, through the testimony to the prison visit | When the song is already a story, play it straight at film scale |
| Carly Pearce and Chris Stapleton, "We Don't Fight Anymore" | The house burns while the couple stand apart and watch | One metaphor made physical |
| Cole Swindell and Lainey Wilson, "Never Say Never" | The on-off couple turn out to be a prison guard and an inmate | A reveal that re-reads the lyric |
| Reba McEntire and Linda Davis, "Does He Love You" | A boat explodes, then the scene is shown to be a film set | A frame story with a double turn |
| Johnny Cash, "Hurt" (dir. Mark Romanek) | A derelict museum; footage of the young Cash against the frail singer | Objects and decay as story; then against now |
| The Dead South, "In Hell I'll Be in Good Company" (dir. Zach Wilson) | The band performs in place while the backdrop keeps changing | One visual rule held for a whole song |

The Dead South video passed 440 million views on that single idea. It is cheap to copy
with AI: same figure, same pose, a new location on every bar.

## Dark country

Subjects from gothic country: crime, religious imagery, death, ghosts, the devil,
betrayal, alcohol, murder.

Camera grammar from westerns:

- Extreme wide with a small figure: personal drama becomes fate.
- Extreme close-up on eyes, hands or one object, cut straight against the wide.
- A doorway or window as a frame inside the frame, for a character who cannot belong.
- Silhouette against fire or low sun.
- A slow push-in on a still figure.

## Lessons from other genres

| Genre | Reference | What it does | What to take |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hip-hop | Childish Gambino, "This Is America" (dir. Hiro Murai) | Six long choreographed shots; calm performance in front, chaos behind | A second story in the background that rewards a rewatch |
| K-pop | The BTS Universe | One storyline spread across many videos, never officially explained | Recurring symbols across songs (not used by Dark Nord: each video stands alone) |
| Metal | Iron Maiden's Eddie | One mascot who takes a new guise for each album | A new situation per song, same silhouette and marks |
| Pop | ISMIR 2021 | Cuts cluster on section boundaries more than any other genre | Change location or look exactly where the section changes |
| R&B / editing | ISMIR 2021 | Cuts land on downbeats, or just before them for surprise | Cut a few frames ahead of the downbeat at climaxes |

Metal videos are performance-led by convention. For a doom-leaning channel, the
mascot's signature action in the chorus fills the role of the band performance.
