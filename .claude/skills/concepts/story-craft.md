# Story Craft for Music Videos

Last checked: 2026-11-12 (source: docs/research.md)

A music video holds attention when the pictures tell a story beside the lyric, and when
the cut follows the song's form.

## The dial: three ways a picture can treat a lyric

From Andrew Goodwin, *Dancing in the Distraction Factory* (1992).

| Mode | What the picture does | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Illustration | Shows what the line says | The title line and one anchor line per section |
| Amplification | Adds meaning the lyric leaves out | The spine of every video |
| Disjuncture | Ignores or contradicts the lyric | Irony, or the moment a twist lands |

Showing each line literally is the weakest option.

## Write a gapped story

Carol Vernallis: music-video editing answers to the music first and does not serve a
continuous story the way film does. Videos show charged moments and leave gaps.

So: key moments only, no connective scenes, closure held back. The strongest videos tell
a second story that the lyric only implies.

A study of 548 official videos (Prétet, Richard and Peeters, ISMIR 2021) found editors
cut to musical structure: faster toward climaxes in verses and choruses, slower in
bridges. Of those videos, 227 were concept, 160 narrative, 74 performance and 62 dance.

## One story job per section

| Section | Story job | Picture and cut |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | Cold open on the most charged image; raise a question | One or two long shots; natural sound can play before the vocal |
| Verse 1 | Who, where, what they want; plant an object | Bar-length shots, wide and medium, calm camera |
| Pre-chorus | Pressure builds toward a choice | Shorter shots, slow push-ins |
| Chorus 1 | The theme as one image that can return | Strongest composition of the video |
| Verse 2 | New information that changes verse 1 | New place or time; higher stakes |
| Chorus 2 | The chorus image again, visibly changed | Same framing, different state |
| Bridge | The turn: reveal, memory or low point | Longest takes; a shift in light or color |
| Final chorus | Payoff; the chorus image at its extreme | Fastest cutting; callbacks to earlier shots |
| Outro | Aftermath; an image that re-reads the opening | One held shot |

## The six story rules

1. **Find the gap first:** what happened before the song, what happens after it, and
   who else was there.
2. **Amplify by default.** At most one literal line per section.
3. **One chorus image** that returns changed each time.
4. **One turn,** placed in the bridge or the final chorus.
5. **One object** that passes through at least three sections.
6. **Channel rules win.** For Dark Nord: no redemption.

## Three concepts, always different in kind

1. **The song's story:** stage what the lyric tells, at film scale.
2. **The story beside it:** a second story the lyric only implies (amplification).
3. **The reveal:** a story built so one late image re-reads everything before it.

Each concept states: a one-line logline, the chorus image, the opening shot and the turn.
The user picks one or mixes them, the same way they pick lyric options.
