# Retention: Keeping Viewers Watching

Last checked: 2026-11-12 (source: docs/research.md)

## What YouTube states

YouTube's intro metric is the share of viewers still watching after **30 seconds**.

- A strong intro means the opening matched what the thumbnail and title promised,
  and kept interest.
- If your top moments come late, move them earlier: audiences shrink as a video plays.
- Spikes are parts that were rewatched or shared. Dips are parts skipped or left.
- Typical retention compares a video with your last 10 of similar length.
  Key moments need a video of 60 seconds or more and 100 views.
- YouTube's creator advice recommends a cold open that starts inside the action.

The most arresting image belongs in the first five seconds.

## The seven checks

Design rules built on the sources above, not YouTube policy.

1. The first shot contains the thumbnail's subject.
2. The video opens on the story's most charged moment, then goes back.
3. Every section opens one question and answers the previous one.
4. Something visible changes at every section boundary: place, time or light.
5. No shot returns unchanged. The chorus image comes back altered each time.
6. The reveal sits in the bridge or the final chorus, and the outro re-reads the opening.
7. After upload, dips and spikes are logged by timestamp in the channel profile
   ("Lessons learned") for the next video.

## How to use in the beat sheet

For each section write: its story job, the question it opens, the earlier question it
answers, and what visibly changes at its start. Checks 1, 2 and 6 apply to the whole
sheet; check 5 is enforced again at the shot list.
